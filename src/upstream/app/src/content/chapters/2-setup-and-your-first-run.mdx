Summing those components across 16 layers gives 6.86 billion total parameters and 1.221 billion active parameters, compared with 1.219 billion in the dense model.</p><p class=\"full\">The result is about 5.6 times as many total parameters at nearly the same active parameter count. Start the MoE from its own initialization instead and the comparison says little, since the two runs sit 2.72 apart at iteration 4. The MoE side also logs a load-balancing loss of exactly 1 and about 32,770 zeros in its gradient against at most 17 for the dense run: the router weights, which get no gradient with a single expert.
